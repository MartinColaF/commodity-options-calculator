How many contracts for 5,000 tons

A contract is a fixed quantity in the exchange’s own unit: bushels on CBOT, pounds on ICE, metric tons in Rosario. Hedging a quantity in tons starts with converting it, and the answer is rarely a whole number.

5,000 tons, market by market

  • Soybeans and wheat (CBOT): 5,000 bushels of 60 lb, 136.08 t. 5,000 t are 36.74 contracts: 37 cover 5,034.88 t, 34.88 over; 36 cover 4,898.80 t, 101.20 short.
  • Corn (CBOT): 5,000 bushels of 56 lb, 127.01 t. 39.37 contracts: 40 cover 5,080.23 t, 80.23 over; 39 cover 4,953.23 t, 46.77 short.
  • Sugar No. 11 (ICE): 112,000 lb, 50.80 t. 98.42 contracts: 99 cover 5,029.43 t, 29.43 over; 98 cover 4,978.63 t, 21.37 short.
  • Cotton No. 2 (ICE): 50,000 lb, 22.68 t. 220.46 contracts: 221 cover 5,012.20 t, 12.20 over; 220 cover 4,989.52 t, 10.48 short.
  • Soybeans, corn and wheat in Rosario (A3/MATba): 100.00 t. Exactly 50 contracts.

Rounding up hedges a little more than the crop, a small position with nothing behind it; rounding down leaves a little unhedged. The calculator gives both, and its buttons put either number on every leg of the position.

In the calculator

Under the legs table, Size of the hedge takes the quantity in the market’s physical unit (metric tons for grains, sugar and cotton, 60-kg bags for coffee, kilos for cattle) and says how many contracts it is. What to watch

  • The bushel’s weight is the legal one, 60 lb for soybeans and wheat and 56 for corn. The grain’s moisture and dockage at delivery are not counted.
  • A hedge sized in contracts of one market for a crop sold in another, soybean oil against soybeans say, is a cross hedge, which the calculator does not size.

The prices in this guide are illustrative, not market data, and its figures are the calculator’s for them. Nothing here is investment advice.
