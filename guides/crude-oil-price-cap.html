A cost cap for a crude oil buyer with calls

A refiner, a trader or any business that will buy oil later fears a rally. Buying calls on WTI futures caps the price: above the strike the call pays the difference, and below it the purchase follows the market down.

The position

  • WTI futures (NYMEX CL) at 70.00 USD/bbl, an illustrative price.
  • Bought: a call struck at 75, 30 days, American.
  • Volatility 35%, risk-free rate 4%.
  • The oil to buy as an underlying leg, short, as the Buyer: price cap strategy holds it.

What the call costs

The call costs 1.06 dollars a barrel, $1,057.09 a contract of 1,000 barrels.

The cap

Above 75 the call pays the difference, so the net purchase price cannot rise above 76.06 dollars a barrel: the strike, the premium and the premium’s interest over the 30 days. Below 75 the call expires worthless and the oil is bought at the market, plus the 1.06 the call cost.

What to watch

  • A buyer of diesel or jet fuel hedging with crude carries the spread between them, which the Basis field holds fixed.
  • The calculator does not know CME’s option calendar for crude, so the days to expiry are typed.
  • Prices must be above zero: the lognormal models here cannot price the negative WTI of April 2020.

The prices in this guide are illustrative, not market data, and its figures are the calculator’s for them. Nothing here is investment advice.
