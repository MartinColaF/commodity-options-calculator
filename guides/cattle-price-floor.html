A price floor for cattle with live cattle puts

A feeder with steers to sell in two months wants protection against a fall without capping the upside. A put on live cattle futures sets that floor.

The position

  • Live cattle futures (CME LE) at 220.000 ¢/lb, an illustrative price.
  • A put struck at 215 ¢, 60 days, American.
  • Volatility 15%, risk-free rate 4%.
  • The cattle as an underlying leg, long.

How many contracts

A contract is 40,000 lb, 18,143.69 kg of live weight. 100 head at 600 kg are 60,000 kg: 3.31 contracts. 3 cover 54,431.08 kg, 5,568.92 short; 4 cover 72,574.78 kg, 12,574.78 over, which would be a position with no cattle behind it.

What the put costs

The put costs 3.131 ¢ a pound, $1,252.56 a contract.

The floor

Below 215 ¢ the put makes up the fall: the net price cannot fall below 211.848 ¢ a pound, 4.67 dollars a kilo of live weight, before any basis. Above it the cattle sell at the market.

What to watch

  • The local cash price differs from the futures. Enter that basis to read the floor for your own market.
  • The 15% is an input, not the market’s implied volatility.
  • The calculator does not know CME’s option calendar for cattle, so the days to expiry are typed.

The prices in this guide are illustrative, not market data, and its figures are the calculator’s for them. Nothing here is investment advice.
