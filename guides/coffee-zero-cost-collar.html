A zero-cost collar on coffee

An exporter with coffee to ship wants a floor but would rather not pay for it. Selling a call pays for the put, at the cost of a ceiling: a zero-cost collar. The question is which call, and the calculator finds the strike whose premium matches the put’s.

The position

  • Coffee “C” futures (ICE KC) at 300.00 ¢/lb, an illustrative price.
  • Bought: a put struck at 280 ¢, 60 days, American.
  • Sold: a call, its strike to be found.
  • Volatility 45%, risk-free rate 4%.
  • The coffee to ship as an underlying leg, long.

The call that pays for the put

The put costs 12.49 ¢ a pound, $4,685.53 a contract of 37,500 lb. Under the legs table, Strike that pays for the hedge moves the call’s strike until the premiums net to zero, which happens at 324.32 ¢. Shown to the price’s decimals, on the side that costs no more, the call is struck at 324.31 ¢ and sells for 12.50 ¢: the collar leaves a credit of $0.79.

Strikes are listed at set intervals. Given the interval your broker’s screen shows, the calculator picks the nearest listed strike that costs no more.

The band

Between the strikes the coffee sells at the market. Below 280 ¢ the put holds the price at 280.00 ¢ a pound, 370.38 dollars a 60-kg bag; above 324.31 ¢ the call caps it at 324.31 ¢, 428.99 dollars a bag.

What to watch

  • Selling the call gives up the rally above its strike: that is what pays for the floor.
  • With the skew on, puts and calls are priced at their own volatilities, and the strike that pays for the put moves.
  • The exporter’s differential to New York goes in the Basis field, and moves the floor and the ceiling together.

The prices in this guide are illustrative, not market data, and its figures are the calculator’s for them. Nothing here is investment advice.
